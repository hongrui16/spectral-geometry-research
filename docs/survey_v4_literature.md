# Survey 1: SVD / spectral fine-tuning methods

Synthesis. Methods whose premise is "W's SVD basis is privileged": PiSSA, MiLoRA, LoRA-XS, SVFT, Spectral Adapter, SVDiff, RLPO/RLMO, and the analysis papers The Path Not Taken, RPSFT, LIFT. Claims split into (i) capacity/speed (top directions hold the useful update) and (ii) retention (top subspace holds pretrained knowledge, protect it). DoRA, OFT/BOFT, Muon do not rest on W's SVD basis.
Already ablated with a random basis / LR tuning: LoRAM (fixed orthogonal, random orthogonal, Gaussian bases and magnitude matching reproduce PiSSA/MiLoRA gains), Learning Rate Matters (gaps collapse to 1–2%), MiLoRA's own Table 6, LoRA-XS's ablation (uncertain), 2606.31813 and 2511.08567 (PiSSA collapse explained by ‖ΔW‖/KL), Multiple Ticket (random masks match RLVR).
NOT yet ablated with a random basis at matched functional change: SVFT, Spectral Adapter, SVDiff, RPSFT, MiLoRA's retention claim, and the off-principal causal claim of The Path Not Taken (their masks vary magnitude and principal-ness together). In all SVD-init LoRA variants basis and initial scale are entangled by construction.

# Survey 2: RL vs SFT update geometry and forgetting

1. RL's Razor — Shenfeld, Pari, Agrawal (2509.04259, ICLR 2026). Forgetting is predicted by forward KL(π0‖π) on the new-task distribution; on-policy RL is biased to KL-minimal solutions, SFT is not. Weight-space metrics (L1, Fisher-L2, spectral norm; R²≈0.34–0.58), representation drift and update sparsity/rank were worse predictors; sparsity vanished in fp32. Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, OpenVLA-7B, MLP/ParityMNIST. Forgetting-vs-KL collapses RL and SFT onto one curve across LRs/batch sizes (R²≈0.96 MNIST, 0.71 LLM); they match on KL, not on LR/steps. No method. SFT on the KL-minimal oracle distribution forgets less than RL; SFT distilled from RL samples matches RL. Mechanism linking KL shift to forgetting unexplained.
2. RL finetunes small subnetworks — Mukherjee et al. (2505.11711, NeurIPS 2025). RL changes 5–30% of parameters (bf16); updates nearly full-rank; SFT dense. Weight-space only. 10 LLMs up to 70B. Not matched on LR/KL. Gradient-masking to the subnetwork reproduces full FT. Caveat: sparsity is largely a bf16 artifact (Shenfeld).
3. The Path Not Taken: RLVR Provably Learns Off the Principals — Zhu et al. (2511.08567; verified). RLVR updates localize consistently across runs (Jaccard ≈0.58 vs 0.4 random) and avoid principal weights; RLVR preserves spectrum (low normalized spectral shift, small top-k principal-angle rotation) while SFT distorts it; Three-Gate theory (KL anchor → off-principal, low-curvature → bf16). Measurements: per-matrix SVD basis of W0 (principal angles, NSS, Ky-Fan norms, principal-mask overlap). DS-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, Qwen3-4B/8B/14B-Base, Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Llama-3.2-3B. Weak matching: SFT comparisons mostly public checkpoints. Causal intervention: function-preserving orthogonal rotations of the base destroy update-location consistency. Sparse-mask experiment: training only non-principal + low-magnitude weights tracks the dense forward-KL-to-base trajectory best; principal-only worst. PiSSA no gain over LoRA, collapses at high LR. No forgetting benchmark; no method deployed. "Provably" = theoretical argument under a KL-constrained step.
4. SFT Memorizes, RL Generalizes — Chu et al. (2501.17161, ICML 2025). Behavioral only; no LR/KL control.
5. RL Fine-Tuning Heals OOD Forgetting in SFT — Jin, Luan et al. (2509.12235; 2508.16546). Singular values barely change (~0.005) in both SFT and RL; forgetting/recovery tracks rotation of singular vectors at spectrum extremes; restoring top-20% singular-vector directions recovers 70–80% of OOD loss. "Rotation-aware SFT" appendix-level, not validated.
6. Retaining by Doing — Chen, Razin, Narasimhan, Chen (2510.18874). On-policy data (not the RL objective) explains retention. KL–forgetting correlation only r=0.52; Self-SFT vs SFT cases with similar KL but different forgetting — counterexample to KL sufficiency. Iterative-SFT reaches SFT accuracy with mild/no forgetting.
Related: rank-1 dominance (2510.00553; 2605.06523); numerically full-rank but spectrally concentrated (2606.13657); LoRA Without Regret; PiSSA/MiLoRA fail in RLVR "spectral collapse" (2512.23165); GeoRA (2601.09361, adapters from SVD of RL-update subspace, less OOD forgetting, 1.5B–32B, unverified); orthonormal init (2606.31813); KL-anchored SFT: Anchored Learning (2605.04468; moving anchor with per-iteration KL bound; SFT degradation 53%→<5% on iGSM/MedCalc), ConSFT (2605.08879), PEAR (2602.01058). Counterpoints: RL Forgets! (2607.04364); Mechanistic origins (2605.28860); RFT naturally mitigates forgetting (2507.05386).
Answers. (1) Off-principal concentration is claimed and measured (2511.08567; 2606.13657; 2512.23165) but only as descriptive weight-space statistics plus one masking experiment; no paper has tested the causal claim that spectrum-preserving/off-principal updates cause less forgetting with norm- or KL-matched controls; 2509.12235 finds singular values essentially constant under SFT too. Not established. (2) Partially: Shenfeld matches final-distribution KL, not per-step functional step; Chen reports similar-KL/different-forgetting counterexamples; nobody has matched per-step KL-to-base between RL and vanilla SFT on the same data and checked whether the gap closes. (3) Open and answerable with per-matrix functional-step tools: (i) does the RL-vs-SFT forgetting gap survive when per-step KL is equalized (step-size effect or direction effect)? (ii) with equal-norm per-matrix projections onto principal vs off-principal subspaces, does subspace choice change single-step KL on off-task prompts and downstream forgetting (the missing causal test of Three-Gate)? (iii) which matrices carry the KL-per-unit-norm leverage that distinguishes SFT from RL, and do SFT gradients projected to RL's low-leverage subspace close the gap?

# Survey 3: forgetting-control methods and delta compression

Topic A. KL-to-reference / retain-set regularization: Approximate Regularized Replay (2512.22337); retain-set KL standard in unlearning (2410.16454, 2512.10150); CoKL (2608.01743) shows full-policy KL penalties in RL over-constrain, conditioning on correct responses gives a better Pareto. RL's Razor (2509.04259) and Retaining by Doing (2510.18874): forgetting predicted by KL(fine-tuned‖base) on new-task data. Functional trust regions for SFT: Proximal SFT (2508.17784); TRAPO (2512.17636); Anchored SFT (2509.23753); Anchored Learning (2605.04468; per-step linear KL bounds; closest existing per-step KL budget method, global not per-matrix). Gradient projection / freezing: GORP (2507.02503), DOC (2509.23893), OPLoRA (2510.13003), OGP (2602.07892), HFT (2404.18466), MoFO (2407.20999), Source-Shielded Updates (2512.04844; forgetting 20%→3% at 7B/13B), hierarchical regularization (2501.13669). Learning-rate-centric: LR regulates catastrophic overtraining (2604.13627); FINCH (2605.20005): global LR η_i = min(η_base/√EMA(loss), η_max), 93% forgetting reduction on Qwen3-4B/Llama-3-8B with unchanged target score, beats SFT-small-LR, L2, WiSE-FT, LoRA, DFT, TALR; very cheap; strong baseline. LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less (2405.09673); LoRA Without Regret; Learning Rate Matters (2602.04998); MLLM forgetting easier than you think (2603.14493). Per-layer LR: LLR (2605.22297), MoLS (2605.05794), pretraining-oriented; 2605.22297 warns layer-wise LR often only beats uniform at its suboptimal LR.
Answer A: no 2025–2026 paper periodically probes off-task output change and sets per-matrix step size. Nearest: FINCH (global, loss-driven), Anchored Learning (global KL trust region), MoLS/LLR (per-module statistics), DAREx-q per-layer q by output difference (post-hoc). Strongest baselines: tuned-LR SFT + small general-data mix; global KL-to-reference on a retain batch; FINCH; Anchored Learning. No direct evidence on per-matrix step scaling vs global KL penalty; adjacent evidence negative-leaning.
Topic B. DARE (2311.03099); DAREx (2410.09344; DARE collapses at p>0.9 on 7B math; rescale 1/q chosen by unsupervised output-change metric; random DAREx-q beats magnitude/WANDA at extreme rates; magnitude wins for large deltas); TIES (2306.01708); Model Breadcrumbs (2312.06795); DELLA; distribution-aware sparsification (2508.06163); ImPart (2504.13237; SVD-space sparsity by singular value, ~2× vs DARE); quantization: BitDelta (2402.10193), Delta-CoMe (2406.08903), DeltaDQ (2410.08666), UltraDelta (2505.13563), DeltaMix (2506.11087), D-QRELO (2604.16940). Functional selection: Localize-and-Stitch (2408.13656; mask optimized on task loss; 1% of params recovers >99%; small models); TALL-masks (2405.07813); Fisher used for merging (2403.09891, 2603.21705, 2603.04972) not delta pruning; 2607.07557 reports gradient importance worse than random for sparsity allocation.
Answer B: nobody has cleanly shown KL/Fisher-selected delta entries beat magnitude or random at matched compression on 7B+ decoder LLMs; crowded at 50–99%; open at >99% or where DARE collapses.
Verdict: Topic A has room (no per-matrix KL-probe step scaling published) but must beat tuned-LR SFT + general-data mix, global KL-to-reference on the same probe batch, and FINCH at equal cost with the global LR tuned. Topic B is crowded; functional selection on LLM deltas open but must beat DAREx-q, ImPart, Localize-and-Stitch at matched ratio on 7B+.
